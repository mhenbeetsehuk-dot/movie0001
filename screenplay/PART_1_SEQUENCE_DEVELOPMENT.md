# Movie0001 — Part I Sequence Development

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Structural screenplay development

> This document develops the Part I sequence framework into filmable dramatic units. Unresolved story facts remain marked OPEN rather than being silently invented.

## Sequence 01 — THE DROP

**Function:** Throw the protagonist into an unfamiliar world immediately.

**Opening image:** The protagonist is in the final moments of a forced descent/drop toward an unknown planetary surface.

**Characters:** The protagonist.

**Location:** Planetary arrival zone — exact geography OPEN.

**Objective:** Survive the landing and understand where he is.

**Conflict:** The environment is immediately incompatible with normal human expectations.

**Action beats:**
1. Establish the protagonist's physical and emotional state.
2. The drop becomes uncontrolled or reaches its terminal phase.
3. He sees the planet for the first time.
4. Impact / landing.
5. Immediate environmental threat.
6. He realizes normal rescue is unavailable.
7. He finds or reaches an underground opening/shelter.

**Visual direction:** Large-scale alien environment contrasted with close, physical survival detail.

**Dialogue:** Minimal. The opening should communicate through action and reaction.

**Turning point:** The protagonist commits to going underground.

**Continuity:** His reason for being dropped remains a mystery unless the story establishes otherwise.

---

## Sequence 02 — FIRST SHELTER

**Function:** Establish the underground as the protagonist's new world.

**Location:** First underground shelter.

**Objective:** Find protection, water and a place to recover.

**Conflict:** The shelter is not safe simply because it is underground.

**Action beats:**
1. Explore the immediate area.
2. Assess injuries/resources.
3. Establish temporary shelter.
4. Discover unfamiliar environmental signs.
5. Hear/observe something suggesting another presence.
6. Decide not to investigate yet.

**Reveal:** The underground contains evidence that it is more than a simple cave system.

**Turning point:** The protagonist realizes he may not be alone.

---

## Sequence 03 — LEARNING TO SURVIVE

**Function:** Turn survival from panic into a system.

**Objective:** Secure sustainable food, water and shelter.

**Conflict:** Resources are limited and the environment has rules he does not understand.

**Action beats:**
1. Failed attempt at obtaining a resource.
2. Observation of environmental behaviour.
3. Hypothesis.
4. Second attempt.
5. Partial success.
6. Establish a repeatable survival routine.

**Character development:** Competence begins replacing panic.

**Visual motif:** Repetition — the same actions become more efficient as he learns.

---

## Sequence 04 — THE FIRST SIGN

**Function:** Introduce the central mystery.

**Objective:** Investigate an anomaly discovered during survival activity.

**Conflict:** Investigating costs time and exposes him to danger.

**Required mystery:** Evidence that raises the possibility that someone or something has interacted with this environment before.

**Action beats:**
1. Discover anomaly.
2. Inspect it.
3. Find a second related sign.
4. Connect the two.
5. Decide whether to follow the evidence.

**Turning point:** Survival is no longer the only question.

---

## Sequence 05 — ADAPTATION

**Function:** Introduce the protagonist's physical/biological change.

**Objective:** Continue surviving despite increasing environmental pressure.

**Conflict:** Something about his body or perception appears different.

**Action beats:**
1. Notice an unusual symptom or capability.
2. Dismiss it as stress.
3. Repeat observation.
4. Test the change.
5. Gain an unexpected survival advantage.
6. Realize the advantage has a cost.

**Important:** The exact mechanism of adaptation remains OPEN.

**Emotional beat:** Fear of losing control of himself.

---

## Sequence 06 — THE UNDERGROUND REVEALS ITSELF

**Function:** Expand the film beyond basic survival.

**Objective:** Follow evidence deeper into the underground.

**Conflict:** The deeper route is dangerous and may permanently change his understanding of the environment.

**Action beats:**
1. Prepare for exploration.
2. Enter a new underground region.
3. Discover a larger environmental system.
4. Find evidence of structure/order.
5. Reach a point from which retreat is difficult.
6. Discover something that should not exist.

**Visual direction:** The underground shifts from cave-like survival space into a vast, interconnected environment.

**Turning point:** The protagonist recognizes that the underground has a history.

---

## Sequence 07 — THE COST

**Function:** Give adaptation dramatic consequences.

**Objective:** Escape a new threat using the changes occurring within him.

**Conflict:** The ability that helps him survive also creates a physical or psychological consequence.

**Action beats:**
1. Threat emerges.
2. Conventional survival method fails.
3. Adaptation provides an alternative.
4. Protagonist uses it.
5. It works.
6. Consequence follows.

**Character beat:** Survival becomes morally/psychologically complicated.

---

## Sequence 08 — THE DISCOVERY

**Function:** Deliver the Part I revelation.

**Objective:** Understand the evidence gathered so far.

**Conflict:** The truth is more disturbing than the original assumption.

**Required dramatic transition:**

From:

**“How do I survive here?”**

To:

**“Why am I here?”**

**Action beats:**
1. Evidence is assembled.
2. A previously unexplained detail gains meaning.
3. The protagonist finds a decisive piece of evidence.
4. He understands that his arrival may not have been random.
5. New question replaces the old survival question.

**Exact revelation:** OPEN.

---

## Sequence 09 — THE CHOICE

**Function:** Give the protagonist agency.

**Objective:** Act on the new knowledge.

**Conflict:** Staying safe means remaining ignorant; investigating means increasing danger.

**Action beats:**
1. Assess options.
2. Consider consequences.
3. Decide to investigate.
4. Prepare.
5. Leave the relative safety of his established shelter.

**Character arc:** He is no longer merely reacting.

---

## Sequence 10 — PART I ENDING

**Function:** Launch Part II.

**Objective:** Begin the journey toward the deeper truth.

**Conflict:** The protagonist crosses a threshold from survival story into discovery/transformation story.

**Ending image:** The protagonist enters or reaches a location that reveals the scale of what lies beyond everything he has previously understood.

**Final dramatic promise:** Part II will reveal what the planet is, what the adaptation means, and why the protagonist was brought there.

**OPEN:** Exact final image and reveal.

---

# Part I Structural Spine

1. **Drop** — He arrives.
2. **Shelter** — He survives the first crisis.
3. **Routine** — He learns the environment.
4. **Sign** — He discovers a mystery.
5. **Adaptation** — He begins changing.
6. **Depth** — He discovers the underground is larger than expected.
7. **Cost** — Survival changes him.
8. **Discovery** — His arrival may have a purpose.
9. **Choice** — He chooses to pursue the truth.
10. **Threshold** — Part II begins.

# Screenplay Readiness

The next screenplay pass should convert each sequence into individual scenes. Before final dialogue is written, the OPEN elements should be resolved so that the screenplay does not contradict future Parts II and III.
